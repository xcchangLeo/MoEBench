## **Call for Research Track Papers**

We invite contributions to the research tracks of The Web Conference 2027 (formerly known as WWW), which is going to be held in Dublin, Ireland on 10th-14th May, 2027.

The Web Conference is the premier conference focused on understanding the current state and the evolution of the Web through the lens of computer science, computational social science, economics, policy, and many other disciplines.

### **Submission Guidelines**

We will use OpenReview to manage the submissions and reviewing. All listed authors must have an up-to-date OpenReview profile, properly attributed with current and past institutional affiliation and conflict information.

Abstracts and papers can be submitted through the [OpenReview link](https://openreview.net/group?id=ACM.org/TheWebConf/2027).

#### **Scope**

The scope of the conference is the Web and how it has crucially enabled new research and applications. While the Web feeds on and is part of a broader interdisciplinary ecosystem, including technologies such as machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, and many others, it remains a distinct scholarly field, with its own research methods, tools, and challenges.

A typical Web Conference paper should have an explicit focus on at least one of the following:

* Understanding, evaluating, and improving the Web as a technical infrastructure; including core Web technologies, standards, and platforms
* Understanding, evaluating, and improving the Web as a socio-economic system;
* Understanding better the impact of the Web and Web technologies;
* Democratising access to Web content and technologies, making it more accessible, fair, inclusive, and accountable to a wide range of audiences

#### **Relevance**

Every submission must clearly state how the work is relevant to the Web and to the track in the first page. Submissions that merely use a Web artifact e.g. a dataset or a Web Application Programmer Interface (API) or a social network – rather than answering a specific Web-related scientific research challenge, are out of scope and will be desk-rejected.

#### **Tracks**

Our research interests are organized in the following tracks:

<details open=""><summary>Brave New Ideas for the Future of the Web</summary>

The Web is changing faster than at any time since its creation. Generative AI now produces a growing share of Web content, autonomous agents browse, negotiate, and transact on behalf of people, and long-held assumptions about who reads, writes, and pays for the Web are being reopened. This track invites papers that look further ahead than a regular research paper can. We seek bold, forward-looking, and possibly controversial ideas that could change how the Web is built, used, governed, or studied over the next decade.

A Brave New Ideas paper should make a concrete research contribution around an idea that is genuinely new, unconventional, or forward-looking. It may introduce a new problem formulation, technical approach, system architecture, theoretical framework, methodology, or other research contribution that could open a substantially new direction for Web research. It may also challenge a widely held assumption or bring concepts and methods from other disciplines to bear on Web research in fundamentally new ways.

Because such ideas may be at an early stage, we do not expect the same level of experimental maturity or comprehensive evaluation as in the regular research tracks. However, submissions should provide sufficient technical, theoretical, or empirical substance to demonstrate that the proposed idea is more than a vision. This may take the form of preliminary experiments, a prototype or proof of concept, theoretical or formal analysis, a new dataset or methodology, or other appropriate evidence establishing the plausibility and research potential of the idea. Submissions will be judged on the originality and significance of the research contribution, the soundness of the underlying reasoning, the strength of the supporting evidence, and the potential to open important new directions for the Web research community.

This track is not a home for incremental improvements, surveys, incomplete regular research papers, or vision and perspective papers whose primary contribution is an argument or research agenda. A bold thesis alone is not sufficient: submissions must contain an identifiable research contribution and provide appropriate evidence or analysis to substantiate it. Authors must state on the first page what is brave and new about their research contribution and why it matters for the future of the Web.

We expect submissions to present genuinely new ideas grounded in the insightful vision and intellectual contributions of human researchers, rather than relying on AI-generated or otherwise unsubstantiated content. The Chairs and Review Committee will carefully assess the quality of the writing, the clarity of the arguments, and the soundness of the underlying justification and logic. We place strong value on clear, human-understandable communication, as well as the meaningful accumulation and dissemination of knowledge through scholarly papers.

**The relevant topics include, but are not limited to:**

* Visions and architectures for a Web shared by people and AI agents
* Rethinking core Web concepts such as pages, links, browsers, search, and open standards when content is increasingly generated and consumed by machines
* New economic and incentive models for creators, publishers, and platforms when traffic comes from AI systems and agents as well as people
* New foundations for trust, identity, provenance, and authenticity on a Web with abundant synthetic content
* Alternative futures for a decentralized, user-owned, or community-governed Web
* New ways for people to find information and act on the Web beyond queries, pages, and feeds
* The Web as a substrate for collective intelligence, democratic discourse, and scientific discovery
* Long-term societal risks and opportunities of the Web, including the sustainability of open knowledge commons
* Critical reflections that challenge established methods, evaluation practices, or assumptions in Web research
* Cross-disciplinary ideas that bring law, economics, the social sciences, the humanities, or design to bear on the future of the Web
* Grand challenges and research agendas for the Web community

</details>

<details><summary>Web Economics and Digital Society</summary>

The Web is a platform for economic activity at a very large scale, from online marketplaces and advertising to cloud services and the sharing and creator economies, and it increasingly shapes how societies work, trade, and organize. Generative AI and autonomous agents are now changing these markets: AI systems stand between consumers and sellers, and agents are starting to search, bargain, and buy on behalf of people and organizations. This track is a forum for theoretical, empirical, and applied research on the modeling, analysis, and design of Web-related economic activity and online markets, and on their wider effects on digital society.

**The relevant topics include, but are not limited to:**

* Advertising auctions, pricing, markets, and exchanges
* Mechanism and market design for online platforms and marketplaces
* Economics and fairness of platforms, search, and recommender systems
* E-commerce, the sharing economy, the gig economy, and the creator economy
* Economics of generative AI infrastructure and applications, including cost models for LLMs in production systems
* Uses of LLMs and generative AI for marketplace design, bidding, and strategic interaction
* Agent-mediated commerce: markets in which AI agents search, negotiate, and transact on behalf of people and organizations
* Incentives, strategic behavior, and equilibria in multi-agent and human-AI economic environments
* Economics of Web content and data, including creator compensation, data markets, licensing, and the value of training data
* Economic aspects and information design of the Web, generative AI, and cloud computing
* Incentives in network design for Web infrastructures and ecosystems
* Social networks and social learning in economic settings
* Economic aspects of blockchains, cryptocurrencies, and decentralized finance
* Digital labor, online work, and the effects of the Web and AI on employment
* Digital inequality, access, and inclusion in the online economy
* Fairness, privacy, and diversity in economic environments
* Economic analysis of competition, regulation, and policy for digital platforms and markets
* Sustainability of Web economics

</details>

<details><summary>Graph Algorithms and Modeling for the Web</summary>

This track solicits original research addressing the scientific challenges associated with graphs on the Web, including but not limited to graph models and algorithms, graph mining and analysis, representation learning, and practical applications.

**The relevant topics include, but are not limited to:**

* Algorithms and analysis for heterogeneous, signed, attributed, multi-relational, temporal, higher-order, and annotated Web-related graphs
* Algorithms and analysis for incomplete, noisy, or partially observed Web-related graphs
* Representation, reconstruction, and subgraph or motif discovery in Web-related graphs
* Querying, indexing, and retrieval in Web-related graphs
* Efficient manipulation of static and dynamic Web-related graphs
* Scalable, parallel, distributed, and streaming algorithms for Web-scale graphs
* Random graph models and generative models for Web-related graphs
* Graph embeddings and representation learning for Web-related graphs
* Graph neural networks and graph neural architecture search approaches for Web-related graphs
* Robust, explainable, and privacy-preserving learning on Web-related graphs
* Graph foundation models and graph LLMs for Web-related graphs, including graph-based information processing and intelligence
* Modeling and analysis of graphs that arise from AI agents on the Web, such as agent-tool, agent-agent, and human-agent interaction networks

</details>

#### **Deadlines**

The submission deadlines are strict and no extensions, regardless of circumstances, will be allowed. Placeholder/dummy abstracts are forbidden.

#### **Authorship**

The ACM has an authorship policy stating who can be considered an author in a submission as well as the use of generative AI tools. Every person named as the author of a paper must have contributed substantially to the work described in the paper and/or to the writing of the paper and must take responsibility for the entire content of a paper.

#### **Maximum Authorship**

The number of submissions allowed per author is limited to 7 (seven) maximum for any individual submission type (e.g. long papers, short papers, demos, resource, etc..). If more than 7 papers are submitted for one type of submission, with the same person listed as a (co-)author, the additional papers submitted after the first 7 by submission ID, will be desk-rejected after the relevant submission deadline. This means that an author can submit up to 7 full/long papers, 7 short papers, 7 demo papers, etc… Please note that industry papers are considered to be either full/long papers or short papers.

#### **Authorship Changes**

The full list of authors, including the ordering, must be finalized at the point of submission. There cannot be any addition, removal, or reordering of authors after the abstract submission deadline. The only changes allowed are the correction of spelling mistakes or new affiliations.

Authorship means accountability for the work. As such, Large Language Models (LLMs) (e.g., ChatGPT) cannot be considered authors. You can use LLMs to rephrase your text, but you are solely responsible for the text in the paper.

#### **Anonymity**

The review process will be double-blind. The submitted document should omit any author names, affiliations, or other identifying information. This may include, but is not restricted to acknowledgments, self-citations, references to prior work by the author(s), and so on. Please use the third-person to identify your own prior work. You may explicitly refer in the paper to organisations that provided datasets, hosted experiments, or deployed solutions and tools.

#### **Formatting Requirements**

Submissions must be in English, in double-column format, and must adhere to the ACM template and format (also available in Overleaf). Word users may use the Word Interim Template and the recommended setting for LaTeX is:

    \documentclass[sigconf, anonymous, review]{acmart}.

Submissions must be a single PDF file: For long paper, 8 (eight) pages as main paper, with additional pages for references and an optional Appendix (that might contain details on reproducibility, proofs, pseudo-code, etc), up to a maximum of 12 (twelve) pages in total. The first 8 pages should be self-contained, since reviewers are not required to read past that. For short papers: 4 (four) pages, including references and optional Appendix.

#### **Originality and Concurrent Submissions**

Submissions must present original work—this means that papers under review at or published/accepted to any peer-reviewed conference / journal with published proceedings cannot be submitted. Submissions that have been previously presented orally, as posters or abstracts-only, or in non-archival venues with no formal proceedings, including workshops or PhD symposia without proceedings, are allowed. Authors may submit anonymized work that is already available as a preprint (e.g., on arXiv or SSRN) without citing it. The ACM has a strict against plagiarism, misrepresentation, and falsification that applies to all publications.

#### **Ethical Use of Data and Informed Consent**

Authors are encouraged to include a section on the ethical use of data and/or informed consent of research subjects in their paper, when appropriate. You and your co-authors are subject to all ACM Publications Policies, including ACM’s Publications Policy on Research Involving Human Participants and Subjects (posted in 2021). Please ensure all authors are familiar with these policies.

Please consult the regulations of your institution(s) indicating when a review by an Institutional Ethics Review Board (IRB) is needed. If your work has been reviewed by an IRB, please indicate this in the paper, providing approval reference numbers where appropriate. Failure to comply with the above-mentioned policy on Human Participants and Subjects will lead to a rejection of the paper.

Submissions that do not follow these guidelines or do not view or print properly, will be desk-rejected.

### **Reviewing Process**

#### **Reviewing**

Each paper is submitted to one of the tracks listed above. Papers that do not conform to the conference scope or submission guidelines will be desk-rejected by the track chairs.  Due to the expected volume of submissions, the organisers will ask authors to join our review committee. It is expected that authors will review papers allocated in a timely and fair manner. Any author who ignores or does not complete their reviews on time will face consequences, up to and including, desk rejection.

#### **Rebuttal**

Authors will have the chance to provide a response to the reviews during a rebuttal period. After the end of the rebuttal period, reviewers, together with senior PC members and track chairs, will consider the authors’ responses to inform acceptance decisions. There will be no interactive rebuttal between authors and reviewers.

#### **Decision**

A range of factors including originality, technical merit, potential impact, quality of execution, quality of presentation, related work, reproducibility of results, and ethics, will be used by the ACs and SACs to make a recommendation. The PC Chairs will make the final decisions.

#### **Conflict of Interest (COI) Policy**

All authors and reviewers must declare conflicts of interest in EasyChair. You must declare a domain conflict for employment at the same institution or company, regardless of geography/location, currently or in the last 12 months. You must declare a personal conflict when the following associations exist:

* candidate for employment at the same institution or company
* co-author on book/paper or co-PI on a funded grant/research proposal in the last 24 months
* active collaborator
* family relationship or close personal relationship
* graduate advisee/advisor relationship, regardless of time elapsed since graduation
* deep personal animosity

In general, we expect authors, PC, the organizing committee, and other volunteers to adhere to ACM’s Conflict of Interest Policy as well as the ACM’s Code of Ethics and Profession

### **Publication and Presentation Policies**

#### **Publication**

All accepted papers will be allowed the same maximum page length in the proceedings (12 pages, of which 8 are content pages), which will be published by ACM and will be accessible via the ACM Digital Library. Accepted papers will require a further revision to meet the requirements of the camera-ready format required by ACM. Camera-ready versions of accepted papers can and should include all information to identify authors, and should acknowledge any funding received that directly supported the presented research.

By submitting your article to an ACM Publication, you and your co-authors acknowledge that you are subject to all ACM Publications Policies, including ACM’s new Publications Policy on Research Involving Human Participants and Subjects. Alleged violations of this or any ACM Publications Policy will be investigated by ACM and may result in a full retraction of your paper and other potential penalties.

Please ensure that you and your co-authors obtain an ORCID ID so you can complete the publishing process for an accepted paper. ACM has been involved in ORCID from the start and we have recently made a commitment to collect ORCID IDs from all of our published authors. We are committed to improve author discoverability, ensure proper attribution and contribute to ongoing community efforts around name normalization; your ORCID ID will help in these efforts.

#### **Registration**

To be included in the proceedings, every accepted paper must be covered by a distinct conference registration, e.g., two papers require two registrations, even if they have overlapping authors. This registration must be Full Conference (5-day) or Main Conference (3-day) registration, at the standard (non-student) in-person rate, payment of which must be completed by the camera-ready deadline. This registration requirement applies universally, regardless of attendance or presentation mode.

#### **Presentation**

Every accepted paper should be presented at the conference. There will be two forms of presentation:

* **Oral Presentation**
  Among the accepted papers, a subset will be selected for oral presentation. Generally we expect oral presentations to be in-person, but may allow a few online presentations by authors with significant travel difficulties.
* **Poster Presentation**
  All papers will be asked to produce a poster on-site. An e-copy will appear on Whova.

#### **Reproducibility**

Authors are strongly encouraged to make their code and data publicly available after the review process. We are encouraging the (optional) use of the “Artifacts Available” badge in ACM’s Digital Library. If you release any code, dataset, or similar artifact to accompany your paper, and host it in a publicly available, archival repository for research artifacts that provides a Document Object Identifier (DOI), you are welcome to apply for this badge. A special subcommittee will check the artifacts of all accepted papers for availability and relatedness to the paper after the acceptance notification.

#### **ACM Open Access**

The ACM Open program is designed to help institutions actively support Open Access publishing. ACM is proud to share that ~3,000 institutions worldwide are already participating , covering approximately 76% of all ACM conference papers. To be included in ACM Open, the corresponding author must be affiliated with a participating institution. A list of all ACM Open participating institutions can be found here. For APC-eligible articles, including research papers, short papers, and survey papers, where none of the authors are currently affiliated with a participating institution, an APC will be required.

To support authors and institutions that are still adapting to ACM’s Open Access publishing model, the ACM Council has approved subsidised APC rates for 2027. Authors whose institutions are not yet participating in ACM Open will pay a discounted rate of $500 (ACM/SIG Members) or $750 (Non-Members). We encourage authors to help bring their institutions into ACM Open during 2027, as ACM’s temporary APC subsidy is scheduled to end after this year.

Please note that ACM is not lowering APC pricing. Rather, ACM is providing a one-year subsidy for 2027 that reduces APC pricing for authors whose institutions are not yet participating in ACM Open. 2027 will be the final year of this subsidy and in the future APC pricing will be based on the actual cost of publishing and hosting articles in the ACM Digital Library. It is possible that APC prices will go up or down in the future.

ACM supports a fully Open Access future. OA papers get 2–3x more downloads and 70% more citations. Please encourage your authors to support this effort by paying APCs and by urging their institutions to join ACM Open.
